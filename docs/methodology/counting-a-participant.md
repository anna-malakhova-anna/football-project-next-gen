## 2. Who counts as one person

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
The same child appears under different spellings, in different leagues, clubs and countries, and sometimes with a mistyped birthday. We decide which registrations belong to one person with a transparent, rule-and-evidence matcher built in SQL: it links records that a source itself says are the same person, compares names, dates of birth, contact details and context for plausible pairs, and sends uncertain pairs to a person to decide. 37,677 registrations resolve to 14,991 participants. See [how identity resolution works](../identity_resolution.md).

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
We are deliberately careful: we would rather leave two records of one child apart than merge two children into one. A wrong merge undercounts participation and can apply one child's consent to a sibling. The matcher's measured accuracy is in [Phase 2 results](../phase2_results.md).


## 3. The counting rules

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
These fifteen rules are the methodology. They are implemented once, in the counting tables, and every report reads from them.

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| C1 | A participant is a resolved person with at least one valid registration in the year. We count people, not registrations. |
| C2 | A registration is valid unless it is an exact duplicate (a double submit or a repeated page) or falls outside the reporting period. A registration with an impossible date of birth still counts: the person is real, only the date is wrong. |
| C3 | The global reporting period is the calendar year. US spring and fall roll up into one year, so US numbers are comparable with Canadian and international annual seasons. |
| C4 | For the US only there is a separate spring and fall view. It is never blended with the annual sources. |
| C5 | A person registered in two leagues in one year is counted once globally and once in each organization's own count. |
| C6 | A person registered in two countries in one year is counted once globally. In the additive country view they belong to the country of their most recent registration that year; in the presence view they are counted in each country they played in. |
| C7 | Age is age on December 31 of the year (for Canadian records that give only a birth year: year minus birth year). |
| C8 | A person in two divisions in one year has a primary division (the one with the most registrations, ties to the most recent); all of their divisions are kept. |
| C9 | Sex is the registration-reported sex. Unknown stays unknown and is reported as its own group; it is never guessed. |
| C10 | Youth means 17 or under on December 31. |
| C11 | A new participant is in their first valid year. The first year of data is left out of new and returning counts, because everyone would look new. |
| C12 | A returning participant has a valid registration this year and last year. |
| C13 | A reactivated participant has a valid registration this year, none last year, and at least one earlier. |
| C14 | Records in clusters with an open identity issue are counted, and the number of such participants is reported alongside. |
| C15 | A count is registered participants in onboarded sources. It is not total flag football participation: informal play, non-partner leagues and attendance are out of scope. |

**Worked example (a made-up girl).** In year one she plays spring in one Texas league and fall in another, then moves to Ontario and plays there in year two. She is **one participant in each year** (C1). In year one she has **two registrations** and is a **multi-organization** participant (C5); in year two she has one. Her year-one status is new (or first-data-year if year one is the first year of data) and in year two **returning** (C12), so she adds to the retention numerator. In the US seasonal view she appears in the spring and the fall of year one, as new in each (C4). By country she is a **US participant in year one and a Canadian one in year two** in the additive view. If she had moved mid-year and played in both countries in the same year, she would still be **one** global participant, attributed to Canada (her most recent registration, C6), and would appear in both countries only in the presence view.

## 6. Privacy by design

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
Each audience sees only what it needs: **executives** see aggregates, with small numbers suppressed; **analysts** see pseudonymous participant-year rows (birth year, not birth date; a postal prefix, not a postal code; no names or contact details); **operations** see full identifiers because they run the leagues; **marketing** gets only contact addresses with consent; **media** gets only people cleared for photos. These are project policies informed by rules such as COPPA, CASL and GDPR, not legal interpretations.

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
**Small numbers are suppressed.** Any count from 1 to 9 is hidden. If hiding it would let a reader work it out from a published total, a second cell is hidden too. Example from the executive share:

**Consent that was not captured is not consent.** Of 12,003 contact addresses, 3,388 are on the marketing list (2,571 are guardians of minors): an address is included only if **everyone** who shares it has said yes, so a guardian's yes for one child is never assumed for a sibling. ## 7. When numbers change

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
Published numbers are versioned. Each year's scorecard is saved as an immutable snapshot, and a log compares today's numbers with what was published and names a likely cause: new source data, a change in who is linked to whom, or a change in the counting rules. A headline number is reissued only if it moves by more than 1% (a percentage point for rates) or the methodology changes, and a person must approve it and record it in the changelog. A recorded demonstration is in [restatement_demo.md](../reports/restatement_demo.md): one steward decision moved a 2025 headline number by one person and was logged, with no reissue required.


## 8. Known limitations and next steps

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
**Coverage.** 5 sources are onboarded; counts are only as complete as our partners' registration data. **Aggregate-only sources.** School and recreation program headcounts (Source E) are reported as a separate line, 'reported headcount', and are never added to unique participants: a count of children in a program cannot be matched to people, so some of those children are also registered participants and some are counted in more than one program. We therefore publish a **combined reach range** (low end: as much overlap as is possible; high end: none), not a single number. Physical education units are exposure during class, not participation, so the voluntary headcount (intramural and after-school) is shown separately. Programs with fewer than 10 reporting in a country-year are suppressed. **Attendance** is not measured. **Real-world validation** has not happened: this memo describes a synthetic population in which phone numbers never change and one nickname list covers every case, so real data would score lower.

## 10. Data fitness by purpose (added in v1.2)

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
A record is not simply good or bad: it is fit or unfit for a **purpose**. Five purposes are scored: count, segment, geography, retain and activate. Every check is one row in a registry with its dimension (completeness, validity, uniqueness, consistency, conformity, timeliness, consent provenance), the purposes it matters to, a threshold and a severity. A purpose's score is the **minimum** of its dimension scores, not the mean (ADR-014). An organization with fewer than 50 scored records carries a Wilson interval and is graded on its lower bound. Counting rules C1 to C15 are unchanged: this section describes how fit the records are for the rules, not a change to them.

| US league, 2025 purpose | Grade | Score |
|---|---|---|
| ACTIVATE | D | 66.2 |
| COUNT | C | 82.1 |
| GEO | C | 82.1 |
| RETAIN | C | 82.1 |
| SEGMENT | C | 82.1 |

## 11. The uncertainty decomposition (added in v1.2)

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
The participant count has three uncertainties that are shown **side by side and never added**: identity (what resolution of uncertain links could move it by), data-quality exclusions (the count if only records fit for the COUNT purpose are used) and coverage (organizations known to exist that sent no file). They are not independent, and coverage is an estimate, not a bound (ADR-015).

## 12. Missingness bias (added in v1.2)

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
A field left blank is a finding, not a nuisance. Girls' share of youth participants is computed on the children whose sex is known (the Phase 3 behaviour, since unknown is never imputed), and beside it the **bounds** it would take if every unknown child were a girl or a boy. In 2025, 36 of 6,592 youth participants have no recorded sex; the known-only share is 46.8% and the bounds are 46.5% to 47.1%. The blanks are not random: they concentrate in the youngest divisions and in spreadsheet-style files, and the descriptive tests say so (chi-square, approximate p-values).

## 13. Coverage (added in v1.2)

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
Every registration count is the count of **organizations that sent a file**. Organizations known to exist that sent none in a season ("dark") are estimated from reporting peers of the same source, season, size band and region, with an interval. In 2025, 26 of 134 known organizations were dark. The estimate is gross (it does not net out players who also played elsewhere) and its interval was too narrow in some years of the synthetic evaluation: that is reported, not tuned away (ADR-015).

## 14. The club-market lens (added in v1.2)

<!-- DRAFT: Anna to rewrite -->
Club-facing numbers are allocated to club markets: **32** groups of contiguous ZIP3s inside a state in the mapping (24 of them have participants in the data), plus buckets for participants outside any market or without a usable ZIP. The markets are a **project approximation, not any club's territory**; a ZIP3 shared by two markets is split by weight, so allocated counts are fractional. No participation rate is shown until population denominators are loaded (they are not, and are never invented). Cells below the small-cell size are suppressed, with secondary suppression where a total is published (ADR-016).
