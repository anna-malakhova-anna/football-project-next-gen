# ADR-023: Product boundaries and the visual roadmap

Status: proposed (Phase 6 addendum; Anna confirms). Synthetic project; every role is fictional.

## Boundaries
- A data product is an output port, not a layer. Each `product.yml` now lists `internal_models`, `share_models`, `output_ports`, `aggregate_ports` and `restricted_ports` (with named consumers). `products/shared_reference.yml` holds models that belong to no product (the answer-key harness `eval_*`, the goal scorecard, three governance share models, the roadmap gates).
- `scripts/check_product_boundaries.py` (in `make product`, `tests/test_product_boundaries.py`): one owner per model; a model reads another product only through its ports; no port reads `raw`; nothing reads `evaluation`.
- **Result:** 213 models, each owned once. 23 cross-product reads were not through a port. Fourteen are now fixed without widening any access: `mart_identity_uncertainty` and `mart_reidentification_risk` moved to DP2 (they measure the master; now DP2 aggregate ports), and `int_participant_household_timeline` moved to DP5 and reads `fct_registration` instead of DP1's conformed registrations. DP1 now publishes `dq_count_fit_by_year` (aggregate port, used by `mart_uncertainty_decomposition`) and `dq_geo_fit_person_year` (restricted port, consumer DP4, used by `mart_market_coverage`). `mart_missingness_tests` moved to DP1 (it tests the conformed registrations; now a DP1 aggregate port); DP1 publishes `dq_missing_concentration` (aggregate port, counts by organization and division) for `mart_missingness_bias`; DP2's `int_participant_year_market` restricted port now names DP3 as a consumer (DP3 already reads `fct_participant_year`, same grain; Anna to confirm). The other 9 are recorded in `products/boundary_debt.csv`, each with a candidate fix. The main groups: DP1's record-level quality models read the DP2 master (to cost defects in participants); DP3's uncertainty and missingness marts read DP1's record fitness and DP2's clustering internals; the DP2 household timeline reads the DP5 fan link (a backwards read). Decision for Anna: widen the ports (fastest, wider restricted access) or move the logic (cleaner, a refactor).
- Ownership choices to review: the person-grain facts and the household/consent models are DP2's (restricted ports for DP3 to DP5); the fan feed's staging belongs to DP1 (it is validated there) with DP5 as the only consumer; `share_club__activation_funnel` is DP5's port (it was listed under DP4 as well; DP4 now consumes it); `share_marketing` and `share_media` are DP2 internals (no product reads them).
- The `p5_*` tables are assigned to DP1 (staging) or DP2 (identity chain) or DP5 (funnels). The duplicate `p5_idr_input_records` was reported in ADR-021 and is not removed.

## Decisions from Anna on the boundary debt
- **DP3 may read DP2's `int_participant_year_market` port** (consumers: DP3, DP4, DP5), on one condition: every DP3 output built from it is aggregated and suppressed. The condition applies to `mart_missingness_bias` and to anything DP3 builds from the port later.
- **`share_analyst__participant_year` is a DP2 output port** (audience analyst, pseudonymized participant-year; the key is a salted hash, birth year only, a three-character postal prefix). It moved from DP3's share models to DP2's.
- **No widening of DP2's ports to DP1** (it would make a DP1 and DP2 cycle). The DQ rows were fixed by moving logic, and the check now fails on any cycle in the product graph.
  - **DP1** keeps what needs no resolved person, keyed by record: `dq_record_base` (no person columns), `p5_record_facts`, `dq_rule_measures` (counts per rule and key), the organization base and the defect log. `int_fan_account_xref` moved to DP1 (the fan feed's duplicate accounts are found at intake from `stg_fan_accounts`); DP5 reads it through a restricted port.
  - **DP2** owns what needs a resolved person: `dq_identity_facts` and `p5_record_identity` (uniqueness and agreement across a person's records), `dq_record_resolved` (DP1's record base with those facts joined back, the same columns as before the split), `dq_identity_rule_results` (the five rules that read them: one uniqueness, four consistency), and `dq_record_fitness` (the record's fitness for every purpose, RETAIN included) with its two narrow ports for counts. The minimization fitness and its frontier share are DP2's too. `idr_probable_merges` is a new key-only restricted port for DP4.
  - **DP4 assembles the scorecards**: `dq_rule_results` (DP1's measures plus DP2's identity results), the source, organization and market scorecards, the trend, the market rule results, the cost of a rule (`mart_dq_impact`) and the league-facing `share_ops__league_scorecard`. The participant-weighted fix ranking (`dq_fix_ranking`, the old fix list unchanged) is a DP4 view; DP1 keeps `dq_fix_list`, ranked by records affected (see below). `int_dark_org_coverage` (an estimate of participants of leagues that sent no file) is DP3's; it reads DP2's person-grain registrations and DP1's organization base.
  - **Consequence:** DP1 publishes `share_ops__league_fix_list` (its one share output port) and its aggregate and restricted ports for other products. The league scorecard is DP4's output. The validator also accepts a product with aggregate ports and no share port.
  - **Check:** every table was compared with a copy taken before the change (315 tables). All identical except tie-break labels that also differ between two builds of unchanged code: `limiting_dimension` and `top_failing_rules` / `top_problems_plain` in the scorecards, and `age_band` in `p5_child` (DuckDB `mode()` on ties). All numbers, grades and counts were equal.
- **Debt:** 23 rows at the start, 0 now (`products/boundary_debt_history.csv`).

## Fix lists and deterministic tie-breaks (Anna's decisions)
- **DP1 keeps `dq_fix_list`, ranked by records affected** (then the rule's severity weight, then rule id). It reads only what DP1 measures (`dq_rule_measures`, the organization base) and carries no DP2 read, so it has none of the five checks that need a resolved person (one record per child per season, four agreement checks); a test (`dq_fix_list_ranks_by_records_affected`) fails if one appears. `share_ops__league_fix_list` is back on DP1 and shows `records_affected` (rounded to ten, hidden below the small-cell size) and `records_basis` in place of the old estimated impact. The participant-weighted ranking is DP4's `dq_fix_ranking`, which is the old fix list unchanged: it needs DP2's persons through `mart_dq_impact`, and the site's example fix lists read it. No new boundary debt; the cycle check passes.
- **Ties are broken the same way on every build:**
  - `limiting_dimension`: the lowest `tie_priority` in `dq_dimensions.csv`. My default is the same order as `sort_order` (completeness first); it is Anna's to change. Scores are compared to six decimals.
  - `top_failing_rules`: impact desc (severity weight x failure share x records evaluated), then records affected desc, then rule id.
  - A child's `age_band`: when two birth years are equally frequent among the child's records, the later year wins (the younger band), consistent with "the youngest child governs".
  - Weighted scores are summed in exact decimals. Before this, a float sum in a different order could flip a score on a rounding boundary (93.125 shown as 93.13 or 93.12).
- **Test:** `tests/test_deterministic_labels.py` copies the warehouse, rebuilds the affected models five times and asserts identical output, labels included. It fails on the old tie-breaks (checked by putting `mode()` and `arg_min(dimension, score)` back).
- **The no-file fix stays first and is an estimate, never 0.** A league that sent no file has no registrations, so its `records_affected` cannot be counted. In `dq_fix_list` the no-file issue (`DQ-TIME-ORG-REPORTED`) ranks first for every dark league-season, and `records_basis` says where the number comes from: `PRIOR_SEASON` (the league's registrations in its most recent reported season BEFORE the dark season; a later season is never used, because that would be look-ahead), else `REGION_MEDIAN` (the median registrations of the reporting leagues of the same source and region in the same season), else `NOT_ESTIMABLE` (NULL). The estimate is DP1's own, from the organization base, in registrations; nothing comes from DP3 or DP4 (`int_dark_org_coverage` is DP3's and counts participants). Today the 113 dark league-seasons split 79 `PRIOR_SEASON`, 33 `REGION_MEDIAN` and 1 `NOT_ESTIMABLE` (a Canadian club with no earlier season and no peer in its region). The share hides a count below the small-cell size (`suppressed` PRIMARY, text "estimated: fewer than 10 registrations not sent").
- **Visible wording:** the league fix list says "registrations", not "records". `share_ops__league_fix_list.scale_plain` is the plain size ("about 150 registrations", "estimated: about 40 registrations not sent", "fewer than 10 registrations"); the field names stay `records_affected` and `records_basis`. The three agreement rules' plain labels read "differs between registrations".
- **Tests:** `dq_fix_list_ranks_by_records_affected` (every dark league-season has the no-file fix, first, never 0 unless NOT_ESTIMABLE, labelled as an estimate in the share, no "records" in the visible text) and the dbt unit test `dq_fix_list_no_file_issue_is_first_estimated_and_never_zero` (the three estimate cases). Both fail if the old default of 0 comes back.

## `p5_idr_input_records` against `idr_input_records` (report for Anna; nothing removed)
Measured on the built warehouse (2026-10-19).
- **Not a duplicate of rows.** `phase5_restricted.p5_idr_input_records` has 16,000 rows from the six Phase 5 channel feeds (2024 and 2025; collapsed byte-identical rows, flag rows only). `identity.idr_input_records` has 29,681 rows from the three modelled sources (US_LEAGUE, CA_CLUB, INTL_FED; 2021 to 2025). No source system is shared and no `match_record_id` or (`source_system`, `source_record_id`) pair overlaps.
- **Columns.** 20 against 30. In both: the matching keys (`match_record_id`, `source_system`, `source_record_id`, `season_key`, `season_year`, `organization_id`, `country_code`, name keys, `dob`, `birth_year`, `dob_precision`, `sex`, `contact_email`, `contact_phone`, `postal_code`, `registered_at`). Only in the Phase 5 table: `content_hash`, `age_group_cap`, `age_at_cutoff`. Only in the Phase 2 table: names, `source_person_id`, season label and term, `region_code`, `dob_nulled`, `canonical_division`, `is_minor`, the two consent flags, `member_registration_keys`, `collapsed_count`. The comment in the Phase 5 model ("the same columns as idr_input_records") is no longer true.
- **Readers.** The Phase 5 table is read by `p5_blocking_keys`, `p5_clusters`, `p5_clusters_probable`, `p5_pair_scores`, `p5_record_identity`, `p5_record_person`, `p5_child` and `eval_p5_resolution`. The Phase 2 table is read by `idr_blocking_keys`, `idr_deterministic_links`, `idr_pair_features`, `idr_u_probabilities`, `idr_clusters`, `idr_cluster_integrity`, `idr_review_queue`, `bridge_registration_participant`, `dim_participant`, `participant_identifiers`, `mart_identity_uncertainty`, `share_analyst__participant_year` and five `eval_*` models. Nothing reads both.
- **What it is.** The input of a second identity engine (the Phase 5 chain) that reuses the Phase 2 macros on different feeds. Removing it is not possible without removing the chain: it is not a copy of the Phase 2 table.
- **What to decide.** The two chains never link across each other, yet the same people are in both: 10,525 of the 16,000 Phase 5 rows (66%) share a first-name key, last-name key and date of birth with a Phase 2 row, and 9,917 of those share the email too. That is by design today (the channel feeds are Contracted, scored but not counted), but a child who plays in a league feed and a modelled league has two identities. Options: keep the two engines (document it), or feed the six channel feeds through the Phase 2 chain (one identity, one set of clusters; a larger change that moves the counts and the identity range).

### Two identity chains: what the evaluation shows (answer key, evaluation only)
Run against `eval.p5_registration_truth` and `eval.registration_truth` on the built warehouse. The generator plans the Phase 5 feeds from the SAME synthetic people as the modelled sources (`generator/phase5/feeds.py` takes its people and league plan from the US_LEAGUE registrations), so the overlap is real, not a coincidence of names.
- 6,054 of the 6,624 people in the Phase 5 feeds (91%) also have registrations in the three modelled sources; 14,944 of the 16,000 Phase 5 rows (93%) belong to such a person.
- The 10,525 rows (66%) that match a modelled row on name keys and date of birth are ALL the same true person: no coincidence, twin or sibling among them. So the 66% is real overlap, and it is a floor: 4,419 more rows belong to people who are also in the modelled sources but whose name or date of birth differs between the feeds.
- Consequence: a child who plays in a channel feed and in a modelled league has two identities, one per chain. Nothing links them.

### Does any published number read both chains or add them together? (report; nothing changed)
- **Read both:** by lineage, 29 models read both chains. They are the DQ models that cover every source in one record stream (`dq_identity_facts`, `dq_record_resolved`, `dq_record_fitness`, `dq_rule_results`, the three scorecards, `dq_trend`, `dq_fix_ranking`, `mart_dq_impact`, `dq_market_rule_results`), the models that use them (`mart_market_coverage`, `mart_uncertainty_decomposition`, `mart_minimization_fitness`, `mart_goal_scorecard`), the shares `share_club__chart_insights`, `share_club__market_year`, `share_exec__roadmap_gates`, `share_ops__league_scorecard`, `share_privacy__chart_insights`, `share_privacy__minimization_frontier`, and five `eval_*` models.
- **Add their people together: none.** Every number that counts people uses one chain: `dq_count_fit_by_year`, `dq_geo_fit_person_year`, `mart_minimization_fitness`, the uncertainty decomposition and the market coverage filter to the three modelled sources; the participant counts (`fct_participant_year`) are modelled only (the channel feeds are Contracted, scored but not counted, ADR-013). The scorecards are one row per source and per league, never pooled; `mart_dq_impact` keeps `MODELLED` and `CHANNEL_FEED` as separate populations and the site shows `MODELLED`; the count of sources on the quality page counts sources, not people. So no published number double-counts today.
- **What would:** counting the channel feeds into the participant counts, or summing children across the two chains in a new share. A guard test (every person-keyed count filters to the modelled sources) would catch it; not added, because nothing needs it yet.
- **Known debt (decision from Anna, not merged now): one identity chain.** Route the Phase 5 feeds through DP1 intake into Phase 2 resolution and retire the p5 chain. It is on the DP2 v1.1 roadmap card. It would give one identity per person (and one identity range), but it moves the counts and the identity range, so it needs its own evaluation against the answer key before it ships.
