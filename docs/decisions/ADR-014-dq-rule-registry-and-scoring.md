# ADR-014: One data quality rule registry, and how it is scored

- **Status:** Accepted for steps 3 and 4 of `phase-5-spec.md` (2026-10-14). Numbered 014 because ADR-011 and ADR-012 were taken ([ADR-013](ADR-013-phase5-reconciliation.md) explains the renumbering; the spec calls this ADR-012).
- **Synthetic data.** Every score here describes a generated world and says nothing about any real league, vendor, club or system.

## Context
Phases 1 to 4 measured data quality in four separate ways: Phase 1 flags in `dq_defect_log` (how often a defect fires), `dq_summary` (observed against the generator's configured rates), Phase 4 contracts (`validate_contracts`, thresholds per source) and, in Phase 5 v1, a rules table of its own. None was purpose-aware. The same record can be good enough to count and not good enough to contact a family.

## Decisions
1. **One registry, `dq_rule_registry`.** A rule is a row: id, dimension (seven), what it applies to, grain (`RECORD`, `ORG_SEASON`, `SOURCE_SEASON`), a SQL predicate, the purposes it serves with a **minimum pass rate per purpose**, severity (`ERROR`, `WARN`, `INFO`), a fictional owner, plain-language fix guidance and origin. 99 rules today: 24 from Phase 1 flags, 59 from contracts, 16 new. The five purposes are in `dq_purposes`: `COUNT`, `SEGMENT`, `GEO`, `RETAIN`, `ACTIVATE`.
2. **The boundary with contracts.** A contract keeps owning the schema, the partner's thresholds and their measurement (`scripts/validate_contracts.py`, `quality.dq_contract_results`). The registry holds a row for each contract quality rule that names the contract and its version (`contract_ref`), carries **no SQL**, and turns the contract's maximum violation rate into a minimum pass rate. It restates no threshold: it reads it. A test checks every contract quality rule has a row, the registry invents none, and no model restates a contract threshold.
3. **The boundary with Phase 1 flags.** `dq_flag_severity` keeps the *treatment* of a flag in the counting rules (`AFFECTS_COUNT`, `MISSING_VALUE`, `NORMALIZED`). The registry measures how often each flag fires, per organization and season. A test requires every flag to belong to exactly one registry rule, so a flag is neither unmeasured nor measured twice.
4. **Rules are data, evaluated by one macro.** `check_sql` is a predicate over `dq_record_base` (record grain) or `dq_org_season_base` (organization-season grain): TRUE passes, FALSE fails, **NULL means not applicable**, so a rule never counts a record it could not evaluate. `dq_rule_results` is the long-form fact (rule x source x organization x season x purpose); `dq_record_fitness` says, per registration, which rules it fails for each purpose.
5. **The purpose score is the minimum of its dimension scores, not the average.** A dimension score is the severity-weighted mean pass rate (0 to 100; `ERROR` counts 3, `WARN` 1, `INFO` 0, from `dq_severity_weights`) of the rules in that dimension that list the purpose. The purpose score is the lowest of these. An average would let a perfect uniqueness score hide a purpose that fails on completeness: the data would be "90 on average" and still unusable for the question. The weak link is named (`limiting_dimension`) and the rules that fail it are listed worst first.
6. **Small samples get an interval and are graded on its lower bound.** An organization-season (or source-season) with fewer scored records than `privacy_params.dq_n_min` (50) carries a Wilson score interval and is graded on the **lower bound**, so a 5-record league with one error is not graded like a 500-record league. At or above `dq_n_min` no interval is shown. Grade bands (`dq_grade_bands`): A at 95, B at 85, C at 70, D below, applied to the rounded published value.
7. **Trend with process control.** `dq_trend` gives each source and organization a P-chart on the purpose score: limits are the prior seasons' record-weighted mean +/- 3 sqrt(p(1-p)/n), so a small organization's limits are wide and a large source's narrow. The classical mean +/- 3 sd is shown beside it from three prior seasons on. `OUT_OF_CONTROL` means "unusual against its own history", not "caused by something".
8. **A dark organization is scored, not skipped.** An organization that sent no file in a season has no records, so no record rule can speak for it; the `ORG_SEASON` rule `DQ-TIME-ORG-REPORTED` does (from the organization registry, `raw.org_registry`). It scores 0 on timeliness and therefore on every purpose.

## Consequences and known limits
- **Coverage caps every organization-based source.** Because the purpose score is a minimum and the share of reporting organizations is a timeliness rule that lists all five purposes, a source where 78% of organizations reported cannot score above about 78 on any purpose, whatever its records look like. That is the spec's rule applied literally. The alternative, reporting coverage as its own measure beside the quality score (as the uncertainty decomposition does), is a design question for Anna.
- **The label-free scorecard separates the extremes better than the middle.** Against the generator's latent profiles (`eval_scorecard_vs_profile`, evaluation only): manual organizations score clearly lowest on `SEGMENT` (88 against 99.5 for disciplined), but struggling organizations score only a little below typical ones (97 against 99), because their defect rates are high relative to the base rate but small in absolute terms. `ACTIVATE` tracks form design (consent timestamps and wording) more than the organization's profile, so it does not separate profiles.
- Contract rules are measured once over all seasons, so the source scorecard applies them to every season (`includes_contract_rules` says so).
- The registry is generated by `scripts/build_rule_registry.py`; the CSV is the artifact, and `--check` keeps it in step with the script, the flags and the contracts.
