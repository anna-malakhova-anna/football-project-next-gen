# ADR-015: Uncertainty decomposition and missingness bias: why the components are not summed

- **Status:** Accepted for step 7 of `phase-5-spec.md` (2026-10-14). Numbered 015 (the spec calls it ADR-013; see [ADR-013](ADR-013-phase5-reconciliation.md) for the renumbering).
- **Synthetic data.** The numbers describe a generated world. The answer key is used only to grade the methods, never to build them.

## Context
Phase 3 gave the participant count identity bounds. Phase 5 adds two other reasons the count is uncertain, and a different kind of problem: values that are missing for a reason.

## Decisions
1. **Three components, side by side, never added** (`mart_uncertainty_decomposition`).
   - **IDENTITY**: the Phase 3 low / point / high (which uncertain links are real people). A bound, label-free.
   - **DQ_EXCLUSIONS**: strict versus lenient handling of records that fail a COUNT rule of the registry. Lenient is the Phase 3 count; strict counts only participants with a record fit for COUNT. Label-free.
   - **COVERAGE**: participants of known organizations that sent no file that season. For each dark organization the median (and 25th / 75th percentile) of the unique participants of reporting peers in the same source, season, size band and region (the band comes from the organization's *other* seasons; widened to any region, then any peer, when fewer than three peers exist). An *estimate with an interval*, not a bound. Label-free.
   They are not summed because they are not independent (a participant can be at once in an uncertain merge, a failing record and a dark organization's peer group), because the first two are bounds and the third an estimate, and because adding bounds to an estimate would produce a number that looks like a confidence interval and is not one. Each is expressed on the participant scale (`interval_low`, `interval_high`) so three bars can be drawn around the reported count; no row and no column carries a total, and a test checks it.
2. **An organization is known only from its first season on.** The registry lists an organization for a season only if it already exists. Otherwise a not-yet-operating organization would be counted as dark and the coverage estimate would claim participants who cannot exist. (Found when the first coverage figure for 2021 was three times too large; fixed in the generator, with the restatement example refreshed.)
3. **Missingness is reported with bounds, not repaired** (`mart_missingness_bias`). For the girls' share of youth the Phase 3 figure keeps unknowns in the denominator; the known-only figure drops them; the Manski-style bounds are the share if every unknown were a boy (low) or a girl (high). Nothing is imputed. For the age-band mix, bounds per band. Where the missing records concentrate (top organizations and divisions) is listed.
4. **"Not missing at random" is tested, not asserted** (`mart_missingness_tests`). Chi-square tests of independence between "this record's value is missing" and the organization's registration channel, the division, the registration timing and the country, with an approximate p-value (Wilson-Hilferty, macro `chisq_upper_p`, checked against known values in a test). A factor counts as associated only if p < 0.05/4 (Bonferroni) *and* the highest and lowest group missing rates differ by at least two points, so a large sample's trivial difference is not called a pattern. The verdict reads `NOT_MISSING_AT_RANDOM` when any factor is associated.

## What the evaluation found (answer key; `eval_uncertainty_decomposition`, `eval_missingness_bias`)
- **Identity**: the Phase 3 interval contains the true number of people in the data in 4 of 5 years. In 2025 it does not (7,642 to 7,680 against 7,418): the point is 3.4% high, the matcher's recall limit showing through. The bounds assume the matcher's uncertain links are the uncertainty; unlinked duplicates are not in them.
- **Coverage**: the estimate is in the right range but not accurate, and its interval is too narrow: it contains the true number of participants missing from the data in 2 of 5 years, with point errors from -7% to +31%. The estimate is *gross*: a dark organization's participants who also played at a reporting organization that year are not missing from the data, and the peer median does not know that. Candidate improvements (not made, because they would be designed after seeing the answer key): net out the observed overlap rate between organizations, and widen the interval to the spread of peers.
- **Girls' share**: the known-only value is closer to the truth than the Phase 3 value in all five years. The truth is inside the bounds in 3 of 5; where it is not it misses by under a tenth of a point, because the bounds cover only the unknown-sex participants (about 0.5% of youth, because a child with another record that names the sex is no longer unknown) and not identity or sampling error. **The bias from blank genders is small at participant level, large at record level**: the record-level tests show blank genders concentrated in the spreadsheet channel and the younger divisions (U6, U8, U10), and unrelated to registration timing.

## Consequences
- The headline count is reported with its three uncertainties, and the sentence "we cannot give one number" has evidence behind it.
- Per-market slices of both marts are added with the club-market lens (spec step 9).
