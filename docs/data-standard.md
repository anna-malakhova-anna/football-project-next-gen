# Minimum viable data standard for youth flag football registrations

**Audience:** league and channel operators who submit registrations (the ask), the privacy lead (the minimization), and the clubs' analytics leads (the quality).
**Status:** a proposal built and tested on **synthetic** data. Feed shapes, field names and consent wordings are assumptions made for the demonstration, not descriptions of any real NFL, RCX, vendor or club system. Legal points are design constraints and open questions, not findings (see [questions for stakeholders](questions-for-stakeholders.md)).

## The idea in one paragraph
Participation data is thin and messy, and the usual fixes (collect more, buy enrichment, share more) are the ones privacy rules make hard. This standard goes the other way: **a short list of fields, each tied to a purpose, in one format, with the consent evidence that decides what may be done with the household.** Less data for privacy; cleaner data for analytics. It is the one artifact aimed at both audiences.

## Principles
1. **Collect what a purpose needs, and nothing more.** Every field below names the purposes it serves. A field with no purpose is not on the list.
2. **Prefer the coarser value.** Birth year, not date of birth. ZIP3, not full address or ZIP5. An age group is never accepted in place of a birth year, because it cannot be matched across feeds.
3. **The child and the parent contact are separate.** No marketing join ever touches the child. The child's name and birth year exist for de-duplication and league operations and stay in the restricted zone.
4. **Consent travels with the record.** What the parent agreed to, when, on which wording, by which method. A parental-consent email is not a marketing opt-in; only a recorded `NFL_CLUB_MARKETING` purpose can make a household eligible for club activation.
5. **Unknown stays unknown.** A blank is "not captured", never "no". `sex = U` is a valid answer, not a defect.
6. **Every field has a retention rule.** The actual retention period is an open question; the rules below use "age-out plus a grace period", both configurable.

*Required* fields must be present and valid on at least the configured share of rows (95% by default, `phase5.dq.standard_min_conformance`). *Conditional* fields are required for any household that **club activation** will include, and optional otherwise: a league that never wants its households contacted by a club need not send them.

## What we deliberately do not ask for
| Not requested | Why |
|---|---|
| Full date of birth | Birth year serves age bands and matching; the measured cost of dropping the full DOB is below. A league that needs exact age for eligibility keeps the DOB itself. |
| Full ZIP (ZIP5), street address | Club markets are built on ZIP3. The last two digits and any address add re-identification risk and serve no Phase 5 purpose. |
| Child's email or phone, parent phone | Minors' own contact details are never shared (privacy policy `minor_age`), and one parent email is enough to reach the household. |
| Parent name | The email is the household key; a name adds identity risk without adding a purpose. (Vendor feeds that send a combined parent name field are asked to drop it.) |
| Photos, medical or health information, school name, team name | Not needed to count, place or contact a household. Photo consent is a separate matter for the league. |
| Tackle or other-sport registrations | Out of purpose. A mixed association sends flag registrations only. |
| Free-text league names, vendor codes | Replaced by the canonical `league_id`: the largest single cause of unattributable records. |
| Any third-party enrichment of a child | See "Estimate, do not enrich" in the Phase 5 README section: estimates are made from aggregates, never by buying data about minors. |

## What minimization costs (first measurement)
The Phase 2 matching rules were run on the six channel feeds twice: with the full DOB and ZIP5 as received, and with only what this standard asks for (birth year and ZIP3). Graded against the synthetic answer key (`scripts/p5_minimization.py`, `docs/eval/p5_minimization.csv`):

| Matcher input | Precision | Recall | Sibling or twin false-merge pairs | Predicted children (true: 6,624) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full DOB and ZIP5 | 0.9998 | 0.866 | 3 | 7,657 |
| Birth year and ZIP3 only | 0.9998 | 0.827 | 3 | 7,906 |

This is a two-variant first measurement on the channel feeds. The five-variant frontier on the modelled sources (`eval_minimization_tradeoff`, which also measures re-identification risk) replaces it at spec step 10, and the `evidence` column of each field points at it. Dropping the full DOB and two ZIP digits cost about four points of recall (more split children, so a slightly higher raw count) and **no precision** here: the automatic rules still demand exact names, so a sibling is no more likely to be merged.

## Checking a source against the standard
The check is SQL, not a document. The `validate_against_standard` macro (`dbt/macros/dq_registry.sql`) reads the `data_standard_fields` seed and, for each field and grouping key, counts the rows, the rows missing the field and the rows present but invalid (regex or allowed values). Map any source's columns to the standard names in a model, then:

```sql
{{ validate_against_standard(ref('my_source_in_standard_shape'), ['grp_source_system']) }}
```

`dq_standardized_records` does this mapping for every registration source (the three modelled sources and the six channel feeds), `dq_standard_conformance` applies the macro per source, organization and season, and `dq_standard_summary` says whether each source meets the standard (95% of rows present and valid, `phase5.dq.standard_min_conformance`). The results are in `dq_rule_results` with `origin = 'STANDARD'`: one INFO rule per field, measured and reported, **never scored into a purpose score** (a missing ZIP would count twice). On the synthetic data, season 2025:

| Source | Core fields met (of 12) | Consent fields met (of 4) | Main gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| `US_LEAGUE` | 12 | 1 | no consent timestamp or method; consent wording only on some platforms |
| `CA_CLUB` | 11 | 0 | no registration timestamp in the return; consent wording and time not emitted |
| `INTL_FED` | 9 | 0 | no postal code, parent email or registration time in the return; no consent evidence |
| `matrix_export` | 11 | 1 | ZIP missing or invalid on 6%; consent timestamp and version missing on 7-8%; no consent method |
| `vendor_reg_a` | 9 | 1 | league name not mappable; DOB unreadable; no consent version |
| `vendor_reg_b` | 10 | 0 | no birth year (age group only); no consent timestamp or version |
| `pop_warner_feed` | 9 | 1 | tackle records sent; league code blank where an association runs two leagues; waiver only |
| `pal_chapter_csv` | 9 | 0 | two-digit-year DOBs; no timestamps; consent in five wordings |
| `school_program` | 8 | 0 | roster only: no parent email, ZIP or birth year. A roster-only profile is proposed rather than forcing the full list |

The numbers come from the current synthetic build and change if the generator rates in `config/generation.yaml` change; `dq_standard_summary` is the source.

## Channel notes (assumptions to confirm)
- **School programs** are rosters, with no parent contact by design. Asking them for the full list would add data collection with no purpose they serve; a roster-only profile is the minimal alternative.
- **Pop Warner associations** are not leagues: one association may run several flag leagues. The standard asks for the canonical `league_id`, not the association id.
- **PAL chapters** upload spreadsheets by hand; a template with the standard's columns and drop-down values would remove most of the format defects at the source.
