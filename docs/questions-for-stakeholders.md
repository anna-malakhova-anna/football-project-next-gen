# Open questions for stakeholders (Phase 5)

All data in this project is synthetic and every answer below changes a *configurable assumption*, not a hard-coded rule. Legal points are **questions or design constraints, not findings**: nothing here says that the NFL, RCX, a club, a league or a vendor is or is not compliant with anything. Facts about NFL FLAG, RCX and the amended COPPA Rule come from the public sources listed at the end; everything else about internal systems is an assumption and is labelled as one.

| # | Question | Owner (role) | Why it matters | What the build assumes today | What changes if the answer differs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Is a club a "third party" for COPPA purposes, given that the NFL FLAG privacy notice describes the data as jointly owned by RCX and the NFL Entities, including the clubs?** | Privacy lead, counsel | Decides whether sharing under-13 household data with a club needs separate verifiable parental consent unless the disclosure is "integral" to the service. | **Not resolved.** The design is neutral: no child-level data reaches a club at all, and club activation of a household needs its own explicit marketing opt-in either way. | If clubs are not third parties, the opt-in rule could be relaxed for some purposes (a policy choice). If they are, the existing opt-in requirement is already the stricter reading. Nothing needs to be rebuilt. |
| 2 | **Which consent wording is live on each registration platform, and since when?** | Platform owners, league operators | Provenance asks "on which wording". The feeds carry a version on only one platform; elsewhere the wording is a code, a yes, or free text. | `p5_consent_wording_map` infers purposes from the captured string. Each row is labelled an assumption. No wording grants club marketing. | Correct rows in the seed; households whose wording was broader than assumed may become eligible, or narrower, ineligible. The funnel's "known provenance" stage moves. |
| 3 | **What is the actual retention period for child-level data and for parent contact data?** | Privacy lead | The amended COPPA Rule requires a written retention policy and does not allow indefinite retention (public source). The job needs real numbers. | Age out at 18 plus 12 months; no registration kept over 36 months; revoked consent deletes at the next run. All assumptions in `phase5.retention`. | Change the config; the job and its tests do not change. A shorter period lowers every count that depends on history (retention, returning players). |
| 4 | **Which intake channels are in scope: leagues on the preferred registration system, third-party platforms, Pop Warner associations, PAL chapters, schools?** | Program owner | Each channel is a feed with its own quality and consent gaps; the standard may not fit all of them. | Six feeds, one per channel type, 2024 and 2025 seasons. Pop Warner and PAL stand in for 2026 intake; the window was not extended. | Add or drop feeds; a new feed is checked with `validate_against_standard` before onboarding. |
| 5 | **What is enough provenance for club activation: a timestamp, a wording version, a method?** | Privacy lead | The funnel's last drop depends on it. | A marketing opt-in needs a timestamp and a wording (`activation_requires_*`); method is recorded but not required (no feed supplies it). | Requiring a method would make nobody eligible today; dropping the timestamp requirement would add up to 70 households. |
| 6 | **Residents of Virginia, Colorado and Connecticut can opt out of profiling (per the NFL FLAG notice). How is residency known, and is any estimate or segmentation "profiling"?** | Privacy lead | Profiling is a modelled purpose, but no feed captures it or the residency needed to honour it. | Profiling is in the taxonomy and never granted. The market estimate is aggregate and uses no household attribute. | If aggregates count as profiling for some residents, residency would need to be captured, or those states excluded from the estimate. |
| 7 | **Is the child's name needed on the platform at all, or could leagues de-duplicate locally and send a keyed hash?** | Privacy lead, data owner | Name is the only direct identifier of the child. | Names are collected (deduplication needs them) and kept in the restricted zone. | Hashing locally would need an agreed key and would lose fuzzy matching; recall would fall. A trade-off to measure, as birth year and ZIP3 were. |
| 8 | **Who owns the do-not-contact list, and is a one-way hash of a revoked email acceptable to keep?** | Privacy lead | A revoked parent must not be contacted again, which needs something to check against. | A SHA-256 hash is kept; the plain email is deleted with the household. | If a hash is not acceptable, the alternative is no list and a re-registration could be contacted again. |
| 9 | **How should a shared metro (New York, Los Angeles, Bay Area) be reported to each club?** | Club analytics leads | Splitting counts invents precision; showing the whole market to each club overlaps. | Each sharing club sees the same market-level figures; nothing is split. Some markets have no club and are visible only to the steward. | A split rule (by league affiliation, by ZIP) can be added to the territory table. |
| 10 | **What market boundaries do clubs actually use, and is ZIP3 the right grain?** | Club analytics leads | ZIP3 is coarse; real club territories follow other boundaries. | Synthetic markets are groups of ZIP3 prefixes. The territory design is configuration. | A finer grain needs ZIP5 or county, which the standard deliberately does not ask for. |
| 11 | **Is a heuristic band acceptable, or do clubs need a statistical interval?** | Club analytics leads | The band is a rule of thumb driven by data completeness; it is not a confidence interval. | The first design contained the true count in 9 of 20 synthetic markets; the revised one in 20 of 20 but was designed after the miss. | A proper interval needs a model of the duplicate and missing-feed process, ideally validated on a real quarter. |
| 12 | **Can school programs submit rosters at all, or only aggregates by grade band?** | Program owner, schools | Rosters without parent contact or ZIP can be neither placed nor contacted and score D. | They are scored and allocated; a roster-only profile is proposed in the data standard. | Moving schools to aggregate counts would remove their children from the resolution and the estimate, and cut duplicate noise. |
| 13 | **Can leagues adopt the standard (birth year, ZIP3, canonical league id, flag-only)?** | League operators, RCX | The standard is only useful if operators can send it. | Each league is independently operated and chooses its own system (public source); adoption is assumed to be a request, not a mandate. | Fields leagues cannot supply stay missing and show on the scorecard. |
