01DP1 IntakeSourceVersionv2.0.0StatusMVP · buildingGradeGrade CGoalsG1 G2

The front door for registration data: a contract per source, an onboarding record, the minimum data standard, and a fix list per league ranked by records affected. It holds the checks that need no resolved person; the scorecard per league is assembled in DP4, where these checks meet the identity checks of DP2. It does not publish any person-level data.

Owner: Data Product Manager, NFL FLAG Insights (fictional)

Who uses it

  • Data steward and data engineering: Onboard a source once, then watch its quality each season
  • League operator (supplier): See what to fix first, and why the minimum fields are asked for

Live quality: grade C (score 82.1)

Allowed

  • Onboarding a source through the playbook and watching its quality each season.
  • Sending a league its own scorecard and fix list.

Never allowed

  • re-identification of a child from an intake file
  • adding a field to the data standard without a named purpose

Limited by league files that arrive late or incomplete.

One reliable person and one household behind many registrations, with every link carrying its reason and a review queue for uncertain cases. Internal and restricted: it holds names, birth dates and contact details, and marketing never reads it.

Owner: Data Product Manager, NFL FLAG Insights (fictional)

  • Operations and data stewards: Resolve duplicates and review uncertain matches

  • Building the counting facts and the household linkage inside the restricted zones.

Never allowed

  • marketing access to the master
  • fuzzy household linkage

Limited by a second identity chain that is not merged yet.

03DP3 GlobalInsightVersionv0.9.0StatusProposedGradeGrade CGoalsG1

How many people play flag football in each country, with the range that identity matching, the counting rules and missing leagues leave. It says how many registered, not how many played, and it never gives one number without its range.

Owner: Data Product Manager, NFL FLAG Insights (fictional)

Who uses it

  • International growth lead: Decide which countries to invest in
  • Executive: Read the global count with its uncertainty

Live quality: grade C (score 78.8)

Allowed

  • Comparing countries, always with the range shown.
  • Reading the global count as 'registered', not 'played'.

Never allowed

  • publishing a country count below the small-cell size
  • re-identification

Limited by countries that report unevenly.

04DP4 US growthInsightVersionv1.1.0StatusMVP · buildingGradeGrades vary by marketGoalsG2

How many people play flag football in each US club market, how fit the data is for each use, which leagues have sent no data, and why families are not reached. Markets are a project approximation, not any club's territory, and no cell under the small-cell size is shown.

Owner: Data Product Manager, NFL FLAG Insights (fictional)

Who uses it

  • Club youth and community manager: See participation and data gaps in my market
  • NFL FLAG domestic operations lead: Tell growth from better reporting, and find struggling leagues

Live quality: A 12, B 4, C 5, D 5 (26 markets graded)

Allowed

  • Seeing participation and data gaps in a club market.
  • Telling growth from better reporting by reading the range before the line.

Never allowed

  • individual targeting
  • re-identification
  • child-level marketing

Limited by leagues that sent no file and by place data.

Full label on the tab →

05DP5 Fan conversionRestrictedVersionv0.1.0StatusProposedGradeGrade DGoalsG3

Whether flag turns families into fans, and how many families a club may contact. It reports two separate effects: how much more engaged families who already followed the NFL become, and how many new fan accounts flag creates among families who were not fans. Counts only, by the age tier of the youngest child; analytics is never activation.

Owner: Data Product Manager, NFL FLAG Insights (fictional)

Who uses it

  • Fan and club marketing analyst: Show that flag creates fans, and see how many families agreed to be reached
  • Privacy and compliance lead: Approve club access and check the age rules hold

Live quality: grade D (worst of 3 sources)

Allowed

  • Reading how much more engaged families who already followed the NFL become, always with the range and the naive companion.
  • Reading how many new fan accounts flag creates among families who were not fans.

Never allowed

  • child-level targeting
  • child-level marketing
  • fuzzy household linkage
  • carrying a youth profile over to the adult
  • re-identification

Limited by consent that is not captured for many families.
