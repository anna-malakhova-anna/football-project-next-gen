Audience and consent

How many can we reach, and what are we missing?

Only 3,182 of 11,787 registered households can be contacted. The bigger constraint is linking families to fan accounts, not consent: once linked, most families say yes. Governance determines the size of the marketable audience: only a guardian's recorded opt-in makes a household contactable. Counts only; nobody here is a list, and no linked household reaches marketing or media (ADR-009).

The addressable audience and the consent gap over time

What this shows. Registered households rise in steps, each step a new group of markets launching, while linked, contactable and active households grow much more slowly; the consent gap creeps up. In plain terms: registrations have grown more than tenfold, but the reachable audience has not kept pace, because most new families cannot be linked.

What it does not show. A marketing list: these are counts, and no household is identified here or anywhere an audience can see. A household with no consent record is not contactable, and no record is not the same as a "no".

Markets: where flag football and the fan base are each strong (synthetic markets)

What this shows. Each synthetic market with enough participant households, by participation and fan accounts per 1,000 households, split at the medians. Retention and engagement play: strong on both, keep fans engaged. Long-term build: weak on both, slow investment. Programme growth opportunity: strong fan base, low participation, grow the programme. Acquisition white space: high participation, weaker fan base, convert players' families into fans.

Design choice. Markets with too few participant households are rolled up and not drawn, to protect small numbers.

What it does not show. Real places: the markets are invented. A quadrant says where to look, not what to do.

Reading note. The vertical split is weak: every market shown has between 10.0 and 13.2 fan accounts per 1,000 households, so the gap between a "thin" and a "strong" fan base is at most 3.1 accounts per 1,000. The axis is zoomed to show that spread; read the horizontal split (participation) with more confidence than the vertical one.
