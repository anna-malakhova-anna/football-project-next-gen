Flag Football Project

Counting flag football's fans

Synthetic data

What this project is and how to read it.

HeadlineTour

Flag football registers children. This project asks a different question: how many fans does that create, how engaged and valuable are they, and how many can we actually contact? It is a governed data platform built end to end on synthetic data (the real equivalent would be minors' personal data), and every claim is graded against the generator's answer key.

sources → contracts → raw → staging → identity → households → fan models → shares → this site

Five sources (a league CSV, a club API, a federation workbook, school headcounts and a fan feed) are contracted, loaded as-is, cleaned in SQL, resolved to people and households, linked to fan accounts under a restricted-zone policy, shared by audience with small numbers suppressed, and published as versioned snapshots.

A five-minute tour

  1. Fan acquisition: how many fans flag football created, and why the naive count is too big.
  2. Audience & consent: how many fans can be contacted, where consent is captured, and the gap. Governance sets the size of the marketable audience.
  3. Participation: the original question, how many people play, and who stays.
  4. Identity and governance: identity resolution (why the household count is right), contracts, who sees what, what changed after publishing.

Decisions and their reasons are in the ADRs; the counting rules are in the methodology memos. Both are linked from Identity and governance.
