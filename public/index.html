Flag football registers children. This project asks what that does for the NFL: how many fans it creates, how many families can be reached, and what to build next, at home and abroad. It is a governed data platform built end to end on made-up data, because the real data would be children's personal records. Every claim is graded against the generator's answer key.

Phase 1 to 4 · High-level analysis

How many fans does flag football create?

Count the players, link their families to fan accounts, and measure what registering a child changes.

  • About 1,264 fan accounts were added by the programme. Counting every new sign-up says 2,019, far too many, because many families would have become fans anyway.
  • 7,430 children played in 2025, up from 1,138 in 2021. Matching leaves a range of 7,405 to 7,437.
  • Only 3,182 of 11,787 registered families can be contacted. Linking families to fan accounts is the bigger limit, not consent.
  • Registering a child raises a linked family's fan engagement by about 20%, measured against similar families who did not register.
  • Girls are 47% of youth players, and they stay as long as boys. Younger entrants stay much longer than teenagers.

Phase 6 · Data products

What would we build, in what order, and for whom?

Turn the business goals into a small portfolio of data products, each with an owner, a label and health measures.

  • 3 business goals become 5 data products. Each goal has one product that drives it; two more keep the data clean and joined.
  • Intake comes first (priority 11), because every other product depends on it. US growth follows (priority 10).
  • 2 of 6 gates are met. The next one waiting is US league grades.
  • Children are protected by age. 23 of the 28 age rules are still assumptions for a privacy lead to check.
  • 16 health measures watch the portfolio, so a product that stops working is noticed and fixed or retired.

Phase 7 · Global outlook

What can we read in the program markets abroad?

Find the signal in messy data from the 22 markets where NFL clubs hold rights, and plan for the ones that send nothing.

  • We can read 5 of the 22 markets. 2 more send player data we cannot read yet, 9 only report club activity, and 6 send nothing at all.
  • None shows a change we can tell from noise. A market would have to move about 51% before we could say so.
  • A guess for a market with no data would mostly be wrong: the true number fell inside the guessed range in 5 of 15 in our test. Those markets get a plan, not a number.
  • Japan and the UK are one more club filing away from being readable.
  • 4 markets are in the first lane: ask them for data now.

Every phase · Trust and governance

Why can these numbers be trusted?

Trust is built into every phase, not added at the end: each number says how sure it is, each source is graded, and children's data is protected by design.

  • Matching is graded against an answer key. At the chosen threshold, 7 predicted children wrongly hold two children of one family, 7 of them twins.
  • Each source is graded for each use. Only 3 of 11 sources are ready for contacting families, though 6 can count players.
  • 67% of consent records show exactly what the family agreed to. Without that proof, a family is never contacted.
  • Each audience sees only its own share, with listed columns and counts under 10 hidden. Nothing linked to a family reaches marketing.
  • Every change after publishing is logged and explained, so a number never moves silently.
