1 · 6 terms

Reading this site

How the numbers on every page are made and shown.

Made-up data
Every number on this site comes from data generated for this project. No real child is in it.Analysts call this synthetic data
Answer key
The true values the generator knows and the methods do not. Results are graded against it; a real project has no key.Analysts call this ground truth (evaluation schema)
Range
The lowest and highest the true number could reasonably be.Analysts call this interval, lower and upper bound
Hidden to protect privacy (fewer than 10)
A count under 10 is never shown, because it could point at a few children. A second number may be hidden too, so the first cannot be worked out by subtraction.Analysts call this small-cell suppression, primary and secondary (k_min = 10)
Club lens and privacy lens
Two ways to read the same card: what a club should do, or what a privacy lead should check. The numbers do not change.Analysts call this audience lens (annotations only)
Planning scenario
A labelled plan for a market with no data. It is never a measurement, and it is never added to or ranked with measured numbers.Analysts call this scenario (ADR-025)

2 · 10 terms

Counting players

Who plays, how many, and who stays.

Matching people
Working out that two registrations belong to the same child, without joining two different children.Analysts call this identity resolution
Family
The adults who answer for one or more registered children, counted once.Analysts call this household
Identity range
How far the player count could move if matching had joined or split records differently.Analysts call this identity bounds (low, point, high)
Attributed
Each child counted once, in the country of their latest registration, so countries add up to the total.Analysts call this attributed view
Present
A child counted in every country they played in. These counts overlap and must never be added up.Analysts call this presence view
League that sent no data
A league we know exists that sent no registration file this season. Its players are in none of the counts.Analysts call this dark organization
Coverage
How many more players there could be if the leagues that sent nothing had sent their files.Analysts call this coverage interval
Entry year group
All the players who first registered in the same year, followed together over time.Analysts call this cohort
Still playing
The share of players who register again after their first season, year by year.Analysts call this retention (Kaplan-Meier survival)
No longer observed
A player who turned 18, or whose records simply end with the data. They are not counted as having quit.Analysts call this censored

3 · 14 terms

Fans and families

What flag football does for the NFL's fan base.

Fan account
A fan's account with the NFL, whose activity shows how engaged the fan is. Every account holder is an adult.Analysts call this fan account (fan feed)
Linked family
A registered family matched to a fan account by email or phone. An unlinked family is unknown, not a non-fan.Analysts call this household linkage
Existing fan, net-new fan, lapsed
A family that was already a fan before registering a child, one that became a fan after, and one that had stopped and may come back.Analysts call this fan status
Fans created by flag
The fan accounts the programme added, beyond those that would have appeared anyway.Analysts call this incremental estimate
Naive count
Counting every new sign-up as created by flag football. It is too big, because many families would have signed up anyway.Analysts call this naive estimate
Similar families
Families who did not register a child but looked the same beforehand, used to see what registering changes.Analysts call this matched comparison (difference-in-differences)
Engagement uplift
How much more active a linked family becomes as a fan after registering a child.Analysts call this treatment effect on fan events
Before-and-after chart
A chart that lines up families on the month they registered, to show what changes around that moment.Analysts call this event study
Active, contactable, paying
The steps from fan to someone who uses their account, can be messaged, and spends money.Analysts call this fan funnel (active, contactable, monetized)
Fan segments
Groups of fans by how recently, how often and how much they engage, from Champions to Hibernating.Analysts call this RFM segments
Value of a fan
A modelled estimate of what a fan account is worth over its life, in made-up currency.Analysts call this lifetime value (LTV)
Families we can contact
Registered families where a guardian has said yes to messages. Only they can be contacted.Analysts call this addressable audience (contactable households)
Consent gap
Active families who have not said yes to messages, so cannot be contacted.Analysts call this consent gap
Market quadrant
Where a market sits on two questions: is flag football strong there, and is the fan base strong? Acquisition white space means many players and fewer fans.Analysts call this market quadrant

4 · 9 terms

Matching and its errors

How the matcher is checked, and what a mistake means.

False merge
Two different children joined as one. Joining siblings or twins mixes two children's data, so it is a privacy incident.Analysts call this false positive
Missed match
One child's records left apart, so the child is counted twice.Analysts call this false negative
Precision
Of the records joined, the share that truly belong together.Analysts call this pairwise precision
Recall
Of the records that belong together, the share that were joined.Analysts call this pairwise recall
Chosen threshold
How sure the matcher must be before it joins records on its own. It was chosen once, before the charts that show it.Analysts call this automatic-merge threshold (T_auto)
Sent for review
Pairs the matcher is fairly but not fully sure of. A person, the data steward, decides.Analysts call this probable band (T_review to T_auto)
Wrongly merged person
One predicted child who is really two children. Counted once, however many records were joined.Analysts call this cluster
Wrong record pair
Two records joined that should not be. One wrongly merged person can make several wrong pairs.Analysts call this record pair
Household guard
A rule that never joins two records from the same league and season with different first names, because they are most likely siblings or twins.Analysts call this sibling and twin guard (hard override)

5 · 6 terms

Data quality

How good each source is, for each use. The uses, checks and grades listed at the end come from the project's own reference tables.

Use
What the data will be used for: counting players, splitting them by age and gender, placing them on the map, following them year to year, or contacting families.Analysts call this purpose
Grade
How good the data is for one use. A means ready to use, B usable with care, C use with caution, D not fit for this use.Analysts call this purpose score band (A at 95 or more, B at 85, C at 70)
Normal wobble
The amount a score usually moves from one year to the next. A point outside it is a real change.Analysts call this control limits (P-chart; an individuals chart is proposed)
Fixable data problem
A family or player lost because a record is missing or unusable. A league can fix it.Analysts call this data-quality loss
Market
A group of nearby ZIP code areas made up for this project. It is not any club's real territory.Analysts call this club market (ZIP3 approximation, ADR-016)
Players in a market
Players counted for a market. A ZIP code area shared by two markets is split between them, so these counts are rounded to the nearest 10.Analysts call this allocated participants

6 · 6 terms

Privacy and consent

How children's data is protected, and what families agreed to.

What they agreed to
A record of which consent form a family signed, when, and for what. Without it, a family cannot be contacted for marketing.Analysts call this consent provenance
Said yes to messages
A guardian's recorded agreement to be contacted. A form that only collects an email address is not a yes.Analysts call this marketing opt-in
Said no or withdrew
A family that declined club marketing or later withdrew its consent. That is the family's decision, and we do not try to win these back. A form that never asked is a fixable data problem instead.Analysts call this privacy loss (consent: declined, revoked)
Unique record
A record that no other record shares on birth year, ZIP code area, sex and country. The more records are unique, the easier it is to pick one child out.Analysts call this unique participant-year, k-anonymity
Pseudonymized
Names and contact details are replaced by codes. This is not the same as anonymous.Analysts call this pseudonymization (ADR-006)
Deleted at 18
A child's records are removed when they age out, and the numbers that depend on them are checked to still hold.Analysts call this age-out deletion (retention job)

7 · 5 terms

Governance

The rules that keep data trustworthy from source to page.

Data contract
A written promise from a source about what its file contains. Every file is checked against it before it is used.Analysts call this data contract
Share
The set of columns one audience may see, listed by name. Nothing reaches an audience except through its share.Analysts call this audience share (share_* models, column policy)
Correction after publishing
A published number that changed later, logged with its reason. A number never moves silently.Analysts call this restatement
Where a number comes from
The chain of steps and tables a number passes through, from source file to page.Analysts call this lineage
Onboarding playbook
The steps every new source goes through, from first contact to regular delivery.Analysts call this source onboarding playbook

Global outlook

Reading the markets abroad where NFL clubs hold rights.

Program market
A country where an NFL club holds marketing rights through the Global Markets Program.Analysts call this GMP market
Readable market
A market whose player data has a counting grade of C or better. It is the only kind we read; every count of markets we can read uses this rule.Analysts call this market status MEASURED (ADR-025, ADR-039)
Measured
Player data arrived this season and its counting grade is C or better, so it is readable.Analysts call this market status MEASURED (ADR-025)
Partial
Some player data exists, but we cannot read it yet: its counting grade is D, no file arrived this season, or it sends counts only.Analysts call this market status PARTIAL
Club activity only
No player data, but clubs report activity in the market, such as fan events and games. It is never counted as players.Analysts call this market status PROXY_ONLY
Sends nothing
No player data and no club activity reported.Analysts call this market status DARK
Counting grade
The data-quality grade for the counting use, A to D. It decides whether a market can be read.Analysts call this COUNT purpose grade
Direction
Whether a market is growing, flat or shrinking, comparing only leagues that reported in both seasons.Analysts call this like-for-like growth label
Change we can tell from noise
The smallest one-season change that is bigger than normal year-to-year wobble.Analysts call this minimum detectable change
Minimum return
The least a federation can send: counts by region, age band and sex, with no record for any child.Analysts call this minimum return template (aggregate only)
Assumed privacy rules
The privacy law we assume applies in a market. It is a design assumption for a privacy lead to check, not a legal finding.Analysts call this legal regime (unverified)
