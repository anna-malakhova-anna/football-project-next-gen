3 · Growth

Is any market growing?

0 of 7 markets with player data show a change we can tell from noise.

Is any market growing?

What this means. Each bar is one market's growth since last season, counting only clubs that sent a file both times. Pale bars are markets whose data we cannot read yet. The dashed lines show the change needed to count, 51% up or down. 7 of the 7 point up. That is weak evidence of a shared trend. But no single market can be read yet, and a common shock could move them all at once.

What this means. Growth is read from counts per club. No child's record is needed to read it.

What to do. To read growth, get more clubs in each market to send a file every season.

What to do. No action. These figures hold no data about any person.

Be careful. With so few clubs per market, one season's change is mostly noise. Wait for more seasons before you act on it.

For analysts

Like-for-like growth over organizations with participants in both seasons (mart_participation_by_org). The noise floor is the pooled sample standard deviation of like-for-like growth over every Measured market and earlier season pair; a market is growing or shrinking only beyond z = 2 times it (ADR-025, ADR-026). The club proxy direction is computed separately and never combined.

4 · The guess

What would a number for a dark market have said?

The true number fell inside the guessed range in 5 of 15 markets.Synthetic evaluation — not observable in production

What would a number for a dark market have said?

What this means. Each bar is the range we would have guessed from similar markets. A short tick means the guess rests on one market, so it is one number. The dot is the true number in our made-up test world, dark blue inside the range, red above it, light red below it. For every market, the guess said there was no change we can tell. In the test world every market really moved up or down. So the guessed direction was wrong in 15 of 15.

What this means. The guess uses no data from the market itself. That is exactly why it cannot be trusted.

What to do. Do not quote a number for a market that sends no data. Ask it for one season of counts instead.

What to do. No action. The test uses made-up data only.

Be careful. The test world makes some markets move against their neighbours on purpose. It shows what could go wrong, not what will.

For analysts

Planning scenario (ADR-025 section 3): comparables are Measured markets in the same region and legal regime (else region, else all); the range is the lowest to highest per-club rate times the market's clubs with rights. How do we make a market readable?

How do we make a market readable?

What this means. Each small chart is one market's counting score by season, the markets close to readable first. The dashed line is the bar a market must clear to be read; a red point is below it. Scores drop when clubs do not send a file.

What this means. Each federation gets its own report card with its top fixes. It never sees another market's data.

What to do. Ask the federations below the bar to get their missing clubs to send a file.

What to do. Send each report card only to its own federation.

Be careful. A score above the bar means the data can be counted. It does not mean every player is in it.

For analysts

Counting score per market and season from dq_scorecard_country (the source scorecard's method grouped by country). Timeliness, the share of known clubs that filed, holds most markets back. The partner report card (share_partner__federation_report_card, ADR-027) is not on this site: each federation receives only its own.
