For analysts

Grades are per use (purpose), latest season. A use's grade is its weakest quality check (the minimum of its dimension scores, ADR-014); a small source is graded on the lower end of its Wilson interval. "Ready" means grade A or B. Checks a use does not need are shown as a grey dash and never count against it. Source order: worst grade for contacting families first.

For analysts

Rules are ranked by the participants (or households, for activation) their failures remove from the use's population, latest season, modelled population. The cumulative share in the headline is computed in SQL (mart_dq_impact.cumulative_share) at the smallest k that reaches 80%, capped at 6. A rule that affects fewer than the small-cell limit is shown as "fewer than 10"; other estimates are rounded to the nearest 10. The cost is what failing records remove from a count, not what a fix would recover.

Is it small leagues, or everyone?

What this means. The weak grades sit mostly with small leagues and leagues that send spreadsheets or use new intake channels. Large leagues on the recommended software are mostly fine.

What this means. The same pattern holds for consent records: small leagues and spreadsheet senders are least likely to record them.

What to do. Offer the recommended software and a short guide to small leagues first.

What to do. Add a required consent field to the spreadsheet template.
